# Secure Collaboration Lab

## Goal

Practice selecting a storage location and sharing method for a small project. Use a test tenant or sample data only. Do not upload personal, confidential, or production information.

## Scenario

Your three-person team is preparing an event. The working documents are an agenda, a budget, and a supplier brief. One supplier needs to review only the brief, while the internal team must edit all three documents.

## Tasks

1. Create or identify a team workspace in Microsoft Teams. Confirm where its channel files are stored.
2. Create three folders or files with clear names and an owner.
3. Give the internal team edit access through the team or site membership rather than adding individuals to every file.
4. Share the supplier brief with the supplier using **Specific people** or another approved authenticated option. Choose view or edit deliberately.
5. Inspect the link settings. Record the recipient scope, permission, expiration, and whether sign-in is required.
6. Remove the test share and verify that the supplier can no longer access the file.

## What to verify

- A continuing team project is not dependent on one employee's personal OneDrive.
- The supplier cannot browse unrelated team content.
- Edit access is granted only where needed.
- An external share can be identified and removed.
- The selected option complies with the organization's policy.

## Reflection

1. What would change if the supplier needed to upload a file but not read the folder?
2. What risks would an **Anyone with the link** link introduce?
3. Who should review guest access after the event ends?
4. Which settings must an administrator control at organization or site level?

## Important note

SharePoint and OneDrive settings are hierarchical. A site can be more restrictive than the organization, but it cannot be more permissive than the organization-level setting. Microsoft Entra guest and collaboration settings may also apply, depending on the sharing model.
